PEBC MCQ & OSCE Study Guide

Build a focused study routine that develops clinical reasoning, knowledge application and patient-centred communication for Canadian pharmacy practice.

MCQ preparation

  1. Review the current official PEBC examination blueprint and candidate information.
  2. Organize study by therapeutic area and identify your weakest topics.
  3. Practise case-based questions and explain why each option is appropriate or inappropriate.
  4. Keep an error log and revisit missed concepts on a regular schedule.

OSCE preparation

  1. Practise opening a consultation, confirming patient needs and gathering essential information.
  2. Explain recommendations in plain language and check understanding.
  3. Work through timed practice scenarios with structured feedback.
  4. Review communication, safety and clinical reasoning after each practice station.

A structured four-month study plan

An illustrative 16-week study framework, not an official PEBC schedule or a guarantee of readiness.

  1. Month 1 — Foundations (Weeks 1–4): review the official examination blueprint, assess learning gaps, and establish a systematic therapeutics study routine.
  2. Month 2 — Clinical application (Weeks 5–8): continue therapeutic review, practise case-based MCQs, and build an error log.
  3. Month 3 — Integration (Weeks 9–12): practise mixed clinical cases, timed questions, and simulated patient consultations for OSCE candidates.
  4. Month 4 — Exam readiness (Weeks 13–16): revisit weak areas, complete timed practice, seek structured feedback, and consolidate clinical reasoning and communication.

PEBC blueprint, exam dates and practice questions

Before selecting a preparation course, distinguish the Pharmacist Qualifying Examination Part I (MCQ) from Part II (OSCE). Use the official qualifying examination blueprint to align your preparation with current competencies, and check the official PEBC examination dates and fees before making a study calendar.

For the MCQ, practise interpreting patient information, selecting a safe recommendation and justifying the alternatives. For the OSCE, practise gathering information, communicating recommendations, recognizing referral needs and reflecting on time management.
