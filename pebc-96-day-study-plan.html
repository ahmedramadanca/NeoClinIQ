The 96-Day PEBC Study Plan: A Week-by-Week Structure for MCQ Preparation

Ninety-six days — sixteen weeks — is enough time to prepare properly for the PEBC MCQ, and short enough that every week has to count. Here is the structure that makes those weeks work, whether you study one day a week or seven.

Start from the map, not the calendar

The curriculum comes first: 14 clinical modules and 161 chapters covering the therapeutic areas the PEBC blueprint weights. Your plan is simply those chapters, distributed across your available days, in an order that builds foundations before integration. Count your real study days per week honestly — a plan built on imaginary time collapses by week three.

Phase 1 (weeks 1–6): foundations

Work through the core modules chapter by chapter. For each chapter: learn for understanding, answer its questions the same day, read the rationales, and record a confidence level. Low-confidence chapters are not failures — they are your future revision list, captured while there is still time to use it.

Phase 2 (weeks 7–11): integration

Finish the remaining modules and start mixing topics: question blocks that cross chapters, cases that combine conditions, and your first timed practice. Revisit the lowest-confidence chapters from Phase 1. This is where isolated knowledge becomes clinical reasoning.

Phase 3 (weeks 12–15): simulation

Shift the balance from learning to performing: full timed mock exams — 200 questions, 260 minutes — with question-by-question review after each one. Track your misses by competency domain and aim your remaining study days exactly there. By the end of this phase, the exam format should feel familiar, not frightening.

Phase 4 (week 16): consolidation

No new material. Re-run your low-confidence list, review your mock-exam errors, and taper. Protect sleep in the final days; a clear head is worth more than one more chapter.

Track confidence, not just hours

Hours studied is a vanity metric. What predicts readiness is coverage with confidence: every chapter attempted, every chapter rated, weak chapters revisited, mocks trending up. A planner that tracks confidence per chapter turns "I studied a lot" into "I know exactly what is left."

When life interrupts

Work shifts, family, illness — sixteen weeks will not run perfectly. The fix is structural: because the plan is chapter-based, a lost week moves chapters; it does not break the plan. Recompute the pace, keep the phases in order, and protect the simulation phase — it is the last thing to cut, never the first.
